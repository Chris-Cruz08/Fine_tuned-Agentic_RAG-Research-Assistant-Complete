## Executive Summary
Retrieval‑Augmented Generation (RAG) integrates a **retrieval module** that fetches external documents with a **generative language model** that produces text conditioned on the retrieved evidence. This architecture enables efficient fine‑tuning and dynamic updating of the model’s knowledge base without retraining the entire generator.

## Key Findings
- **Definition** – RAG couples information retrieval and text generation, allowing the system’s internal knowledge to be altered efficiently through the retrieval component rather than full model re‑training.  
- **Primary Challenge** – The most frequently cited vulnerability is **RAG poisoning**, where malicious or low‑quality documents inserted into the retrieval corpus can corrupt the generated output.

## Methodology Analysis
The core methodology of RAG consists of two stages:

| Stage | Function | Typical Implementation |
|-------|----------|------------------------|
| **Retrieval** | Searches a large external corpus (e.g., vector store, knowledge base) for passages relevant to the input query. | Dense vector similarity search, BM25, or hybrid approaches. |
| **Generation** | Conditions a language model on the retrieved passages to produce a response. | Encoder‑decoder or decoder‑only transformers fine‑tuned on concatenated query‑passage inputs. |

Key properties derived from the provided answers:

1. **Modular Knowledge Update** – Because the knowledge resides in the retrieval index, updating the corpus instantly reflects in generated answers without re‑training the generator.  
2. **Fine‑tuning Flexibility** – The generator can be fine‑tuned on downstream tasks while still leveraging the external knowledge source, preserving adaptability.

## Limitations and Future Directions
| Limitation | Description | Potential Research Direction |
|------------|-------------|------------------------------|
| **RAG Poisoning** | Insertion of adversarial or erroneous documents into the retrieval store can lead to misleading or harmful generations. | Develop robust corpus sanitization, provenance tracking, and retrieval‑time verification mechanisms. |
| **Retrieval Quality Dependency** | Generation quality is bounded by the relevance and accuracy of retrieved passages. | Explore hybrid retrieval (semantic + lexical), dynamic relevance feedback, and multi‑hop retrieval strategies. |
| **Scalability of Indexes** | Large corpora demand efficient indexing and low‑latency search. | Investigate hierarchical or compressed vector indexes and hardware‑accelerated search. |
| **Alignment Between Retriever and Generator** | Mismatch in representation spaces can reduce the usefulness of retrieved text. | Joint training of retriever and generator embeddings to improve end‑to‑end coherence. |
